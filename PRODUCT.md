# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Rhythm game enthusiasts and beatmap creators. They are players who want to challenge themselves with custom beatmaps, and creators who want to design and share their own maps. Context: sitting at a desktop computer, headphones on, focused on timing and rhythm. The primary task is either creating beatmaps in the editor or playing them in the game.

## Product Purpose

BeatSmith is a browser-based rhythm game editor and player, inspired by MCosu/osu! standard mode. Users upload songs, the app detects beats, they create beatmaps by placing circles, sliders, and spinners on a timeline, then play them with mouse input. All beatmaps are public and shared, with per-map leaderboards and grade rankings (SSS/SS/S/A/B). Success means a smooth, responsive gameplay experience where timing feels precise and editing feels intuitive.

## Brand Personality

Cyberpunk energy. Three words: **electric**, **precise**, **intense**. The interface should feel like a neon-lit arcade in a futuristic city, not a corporate tool. The energy is focused, not chaotic; every glow and pulse serves the rhythm.

## Anti-references

- **Boring SaaS dashboards**: white backgrounds, blue primary buttons, soulless data tables. BeatSmith must never feel like managing spreadsheets.
- **Over-designed AI-generated pages**: gradient text, decorative glassmorphism, hero sections that say nothing. Every visual element must serve gameplay or editing.
- **Minimalist tool aesthetics**: Notion/Feishu-level restraint. BeatSmith is loud and alive, not quietly productive.
- **Low-quality game UIs**: Flashy 4399-style graphics, clashing colors, childish fonts. BeatSmith is sleek and mature, not cheap.

## Design Principles

1. **Timing is everything**: The interface must feel responsive and precise. Visual feedback for every interaction should be immediate. No lag, no ambiguity.
2. **Neon with purpose**: Glows, pulses, and color accents highlight interactive elements and game feedback. Decoration without function is noise.
3. **Editor and player share one language**: The same visual system powers both the editing timeline and the gameplay canvas. Transitions between modes should feel seamless.
4. **Dark by nature, not by trend**: Dark theme because rhythm games are played in focused, dim environments. The darkness is the canvas; light is the signal.
5. **Public by default**: Every beatmap is a shared creation. The UI should celebrate community and competition, not hide behind privacy walls.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA contrast for all text (4.5:1 minimum)
- `prefers-reduced-motion` support: disable particle effects and transitions, keep gameplay functional
- Keyboard navigation for editor and menus (gameplay is mouse-only by design)
- Color is never the sole indicator of state; grades use text labels alongside colors
- Consider color blindness: grade colors should be distinguishable beyond hue alone
